Strategy — the bet, the insight, the bar

The rest of this doc is the execution — screens, data, scope. This is the altitude: why this wins, the one risk that kills it, how big the prize is, and the single number we steer by. If we disagree here, nothing downstream matters.

The bet
Show every student a proud, personal, shareable story of their year → they come back in January, and their shared card sells Shikho to the exact peers we want next.

It’s the Spotify Wrapped playbook — identity, pride, social proof — moved from “your songs” to “your effort.” Two returns from one artifact: retention (a reason to re-engage when the new class starts) and acquisition (authentic, student-made proof shown to other students). Opening it to free and trial students too widens the acquisition edge: a free student who shares a proud card is both a near-conversion and a billboard.

Why it works — the insight

Students systematically underweight their own effort. A year of work is invisible to them by December. Reflecting it back — 187 days, 142 hours, a 23-day streak — converts invisible effort into visible pride, and pride is the emotional fuel for returning. And a card carrying real numbers from a real student is the most credible ad we can put in front of their friends — who are precisely our target market, because students buy from students.

The learner-type stamp is the differentiator, not the stats. “I studied 142 hours” is a number; “I’m a রাতজাগা পড়ুয়া (Night Owl)” — or a পরিশ্রমী (Hard Worker) — is an identity, and identity is what people share. Statistics inform; identity travels. (Night Owl needs the hour-band in O6; if it can’t be sourced, the flagship example falls back to an always-computable axis — §6 badges & §4.)

The insight most teams miss — and the one rule everything obeys
We named it a “marksheet.” Marks imply judgment. Wrapped works because no one feels bad.

If a struggling student opens a marksheet and sees low marks or a poor rank, we manufacture shame and push away the exact students we’re trying to retain — the opposite of the goal. So the non-negotiable principle: every student must finish the wrap feeling proud.

This isn’t a slogan — it changes real decisions: lead with effort, not outcome; never show rank or “weakest subject” to a struggling student; encouraging copy for the quiet; tier copy that is always positively framed; hide-name ON by default on shares. Because everyone now gets a wrap, this rule is more load-bearing, not less — the no-shame display groups in §6 are how one experience serves a top student and a barely-active one without either feeling judged.

The one number — and the guardrails

North Star
January-2027 return-lift: got-banner vs a randomized hold-out (intent-to-treat), read on the whole cohort and again on the paid sub-cohort

Inputs we steer weekly: open → finish → share. We don’t optimise a vanity metric; we optimise whether reflected pride brings students back.

Clean read — the confound: opening is voluntary, so openers are already more engaged; a raw “finishers vs non-openers” gap overstates lift. We debias with a randomized hold-out — suppress the banner for a small random slice and compare intent-to-treat (got-banner vs held-out), not just openers vs non-openers.

Guardrail · no shame
Low-group finish-rate ≥ overall finish-rate. If strugglers bail before the end, the experience is judging them — fix before scaling. This is our shame-sensor.
Guardrail · no harm
Zero rise in opt-outs / complaints; no privacy incident. A viral loop that leaks a minor’s data is a loss, not a win.
Guardrail · honest shares
Shared cards are accurate & privacy-safe by default (QA gate + name-hidden). We never buy reach with a wrong or over-exposing card.

What we’re really testing (so we learn, not just ship)

  • Does pride drive return? — the North Star A/B: got-banner vs a held-out comparable group in January.
  • Does the hook drive the tap? — banner copy A (personal number) vs C (generic). The tap is the whole funnel’s gate.

2.1 What we want to show — content checklist for the UI team

The Wrap turns a student’s year into a short, proud story. This is exactly what each student sees, and why. Full interactive reference: ▶ prototype ?v=b

  • Who they are — name · class · section — so it feels like theirs from screen one.
  • How much they studied — total hours shown as school days (e.g. ১৪২ ঘণ্টা → ২৪ স্কুল দিন), so the number means something.
  • How regular they were — active days + longest streak on a calendar grid — the backbone of the pride.
  • Live classes — joined vs held, with an attendance title (ফার্স্ট বেঞ্চার) — rewards showing up.
  • Quizzes — how many, rising through the year, with an “almost a champion” nudge.
  • Video — minutes watched shown as movies (e.g. ২,৮৬০ মিনিট → ২৪টা সিনেমা) — a scale they feel.
  • What kind of learner they were — one rubber-stamp personality (নিয়মিত পড়ুয়া…) — the “I learned something about myself” moment, different per student.
  • Their best subject — from quiz/exam accuracy — specific and personal.
  • Their badge collection — “তোমার অর্জন”: every badge earned, together, with a count headline.
  • A marksheet to share — all of it on one Shikho card, with a hide-name toggle — the viral payoff.
  • Returning students: years with Shikho (loyalty).  Quiet students: the same wrap, with encouraging copy instead of empty charts — nobody feels bad.

Backend & data pipeline

We calculate each student’s whole wrap ahead of time, not when they open the app. A nightly Airflow job computes every student’s numbers for the window 1 Jan → 20 Dec 2026 and stores each as one row. On open, the app simply reads that ready-made row — instantly; no live maths. (The heavy joins on every tap, for the whole base, would be far too slow.) The run on the night of 20 Dec produces the final version that goes live 26–31 Dec.

4.1 Where the data comes from (provenance)

The wrap reads one warehouse — datafuse (Postgres) — fed by the whole platform. It never touches the source services directly, so it can’t slow the app, exams or live classes.

  • Live event stream. Each microservice (exam, live-exam, academic-program, live-class…) emits domain events over NATS JetStream / gRPC; the KPI service mirrors them into datafuse.
  • Nightly aggregation (Airflow, data_pipeline_bronze). DAGs roll raw activity into user_activity_duration_agg (7 activity-type durations), mcq_user_*_daily (quiz / question / subject), every morning ~06:00–07:00 BD.
  • Operational stores not yet in the warehouse (MongoDB): AI chats, model-test detail, QR scans. These are the GET-sources — §4.5 pipes them in.

4.3 Per-task computation — the queries

All queries run on datafuse over 2026-01-01 → 2026-12-20, scoped to the cohort from t1. Most assemble tables that already exist; verify column names marked ? with information_schema before coding.

-- t1 · cohort — EVERYONE (paid, trial, free); exclude only staff/test/deleted.
-- One row per student at their PRIMARY program (most-active 2026, tie → most recent). Free/no-enrol → program '0'.
CREATE TEMP TABLE cohort AS
SELECT s.id AS user_id,
       COALESCE(p.primary_program, '0') AS program_id,
       CASE WHEN paid.ok  THEN 'paid'
            WHEN trial.ok THEN 'trial' ELSE 'free' END AS account_type
FROM "Students" s
LEFT JOIN primary_program p ON p.user_id = s.id     -- most-active program in 2026
WHERE s.is_staff IS NOT TRUE AND s.is_test IS NOT TRUE
  AND s.deleted_at IS NULL
  AND s.created_at <= '2026-12-20';     -- accounts created after cut-off have no 2026 window → no wrap (W-E4)
-- "paid"/"trial" flags: enrolment of that type whose coverage OVERLAPS the window (not payment-date-in-window).
-- These only set account_type + loyalty; they are NOT an eligibility gate (everyone is in the cohort).

-- t2 · engagement
SELECT user_id,
  SUM(recorded_videos_duration + animated_videos_duration + live_class_duration
    + quiz_duration + live_exam_duration + model_test_duration + smart_note_duration) AS total_study_sec,
  SUM(recorded_videos_duration + animated_videos_duration) AS video_sec,
  SUM(recorded_videos_count + animated_videos_count)       AS video_count,
  SUM(quiz_count) AS quiz_count_agg
FROM user_activity_duration_agg
WHERE activity_date BETWEEN '2026-01-01' AND '2026-12-20'
GROUP BY user_id;   -- study_by_month[12]: add date_trunc('month',activity_date); by_type: one SUM per *_duration

-- t3 · attendance (min-stay = 10 min OR 30% of class; held scoped to on/after the student's join date — W-AT1)
WITH att AS (            -- aggregate rejoins BEFORE the min-stay test; MAX() de-fans co-taught classes
  SELECT sa.user_id, ta.content_id, SUM(sa.duration) stay, MAX(ta.duration) class_len
  FROM student_attendees sa
  JOIN teacher_attendees ta ON ta.content_id = COALESCE(NULLIF(sa.content_id,''), sa.room_id)
  GROUP BY sa.user_id, ta.content_id),
joined AS (SELECT user_id, COUNT(*) joined FROM att
  WHERE stay >= GREATEST(600, 0.30*class_len) GROUP BY user_id),
held AS (SELECT c.user_id, COUNT(*) held FROM cohort c
  JOIN enrolment e ON e.user_id=c.user_id AND e.program_id=c.program_id
  JOIN lessons l ON l.academic_program_id=c.program_id AND l.content_type='LiveClass'
    AND l.start_time BETWEEN GREATEST(e.enrolment_start,'2026-01-01') AND '2026-12-20'
  GROUP BY c.user_id)
SELECT c.user_id, j.joined, h.held, LEAST(j.joined::numeric/NULLIF(h.held,0),1) rate
FROM cohort c LEFT JOIN joined j USING(user_id) LEFT JOIN held h USING(user_id);

-- t5 · active days + longest streak (gaps-and-islands; Asia/Dhaka dates — W-T1)
WITH d AS (SELECT DISTINCT user_id, activity_date FROM user_activity_duration_agg
         WHERE activity_date BETWEEN '2026-01-01' AND '2026-12-20' ),  -- UNION AI/QR days once piped
g AS (SELECT user_id, activity_date,
     activity_date - (ROW_NUMBER() OVER(PARTITION BY user_id ORDER BY activity_date))::int grp FROM d),
runs AS (SELECT user_id, grp, COUNT(*) len, MIN(activity_date) s, MAX(activity_date) e
      FROM g GROUP BY user_id, grp)
SELECT DISTINCT ON(user_id) user_id, len longest_streak, s streak_start, e streak_end
FROM runs ORDER BY user_id, len DESC;
-- active_days = COUNT(DISTINCT activity_date); avg_days_per_week = active_days / weeks_since_first_activity (W-LT2)

-- t6 · best / weakest subject (subject-native accuracy — no time rebuild)
WITH subj AS (SELECT user_id, subject_name,
    SUM(total_correct)::numeric/NULLIF(SUM(total_questions),0) accuracy, SUM(total_questions) q
  FROM mcq_user_chapter_daily
  WHERE «day»? BETWEEN '2026-01-01' AND '2026-12-20'
    AND lower(subject_name) NOT IN ('orientation','orientation and guideline')
  GROUP BY user_id, subject_name)
SELECT DISTINCT ON(user_id) user_id, subject_name top_subject, accuracy
FROM subj WHERE q >= :min_questions ORDER BY user_id, accuracy DESC;  -- weakest = ORDER BY accuracy ASC

t7b · Learner-type stamp — cohort-relative, self-balancing

One stamp per student, chosen relative to the cohort, so the types spread ~evenly whatever the raw distribution looks like (§7.1 R1):

  1. Per student compute each axis: rhythm=active_days/weeks · intensity=total_study_sec · clock=night_share (needs an hour-band; drop if absent — O6) · method=practice_share vs video_share · focus=top_subject_share.

  3. Stamp = the axis of largest |z|, mapped via wrap_type_rule (z>0 → high label, z<0 → its opposite).
  4. If every |z| < 0.5 → balanced (ভারসাম্যপূর্ণ). Nobody gets a blank. Deterministic tie-break by config priority, so reruns are stable.

Decisions & grounding

7.1 Key design choices (these remove the remaining unknowns)

Three choices make the design correct under every outcome, so the open numbers only need tuning, never a go/no-go.

R1 · Learner-type → cohort-relative, even by construction
Design: z-score each axis per student; the stamp = the axis where the student is furthest from the cohort median. Because it’s relative, stamps spread ~evenly no matter how skewed any single raw metric is. Branches: even spread → ship · one axis dominates → drop it from the arg-max set (config) · all flat → Balanced. All three ship on time.
R2 · Best subject → subject-native accuracy, not time reconstruction
Design: strongest subject = highest accuracy in mcq_user_chapter_daily grouped by subject_name (filter “orientation”, require ≥ min questions) — a real subject column, no 3-log rebuild. Exactly SC-1737’s method. Fallback: too few graded questions → “most-practised subject” by question count, still subject-native. Time-by-subject is demoted to an optional slide.
R3 · AI/QR active days → additive, never load-bearing
Design: active_days ships from the warehouse set alone; AI/QR/eBook days are a bonus union that only increases the number if piped. Branch: not piped by 10 Dec → number is class/quiz/video-based; feature unaffected, copy is true either way.
